So let's go back to the question. Why did an entire country decide, all at once, that it needed to be fixed?

It started with an idea from those revival tents: you're not stuck. You can change. And if you can change yourself, you can change the world. Temperance, prisons, schools, women's rights, even the weird utopias. They all run on that same fuel.

But notice the difference with abolition. Most reforms asked Americans to change their habits. Abolition asked them to give up an entire economic system. By 1860, enslaved people were counted as property worth more than all the country's railroads and factories combined.

You can talk a man out of drinking whiskey. It's a lot harder to talk a country out of its most valuable property.

And that's why the fever didn't break. It split the country in two. But that's a story for next time.
