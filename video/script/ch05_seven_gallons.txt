Okay. Back to the people trying to fix things. And the first thing on a lot of lists was booze.

Because here's a number that sounds made up. Around 1830, the average American adult drank about seven gallons of pure alcohol a year. That's roughly three times what Americans drink today.

Whiskey was cheaper than milk. Some workers got a daily ration of alcohol as part of their pay. Politicians rolled out barrels of liquor to win votes. People drank at breakfast.

And reformers saw the damage everywhere: poverty, violence at home, men drinking away the family's paycheck. Preachers like Lyman Beecher gave terrifying sermons about "Demon Rum." Factory owners jumped on board too, because a sober worker shows up on time.

This was the Temperance Movement. Groups like the American Temperance Society got hundreds of thousands of people to sign a pledge to stop drinking. And in 1851, Maine passed the first statewide ban on selling alcohol.

And here's the wild part. It actually worked. By the 1840s, the amount Americans drank had dropped by more than half. That's one of the biggest behavior changes in American history, and it started with people signing a piece of paper.
