Picture a field in upstate New York. Thousands of people packed under tents. People crying. People shouting. People falling to the ground. Some of these meetings go on for days.

This is the Second Great Awakening, a massive wave of religious revival that swept the country in the early 1800s. And its biggest star was a former lawyer named Charles Grandison Finney.

Now, to get why Finney was such a big deal, you need to know what he was up against. The older Puritan style of Christianity taught predestination. Basically, God already decided before you were born whether you're saved or not. Nothing you do changes it. Kind of a bummer.

Finney flipped that. He preached that salvation was a choice. You could choose to be saved, today, right now.

And he sold it like a pro. He put an "anxious bench" right up front, where people wrestling with their sins would sit in front of the whole crowd while everyone prayed over them. It was part church service, part concert, part reality show.

The region of western New York got hit with so many revivals that people started calling it the Burned-Over District. The idea was that the fire of religion had swept through so many times there was nobody left to convert.

But here's the thing. Finney didn't stop at "you can fix your soul." He took it one step further. If you can perfect yourself, then you can perfect society too. And if you can, then God expects you to.

That's the spark. Remember it, because everything else in this video is that spark catching fire.
