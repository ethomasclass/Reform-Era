Here's a fun one, because you're living inside it right now.

In the early 1800s, school was a mess. If your family was rich, you got a tutor. If not, maybe you got a one-room schoolhouse for a few months a year, when you weren't needed on the farm.

Horace Mann, the head of education in Massachusetts, thought that was dangerous. More and more men could vote now. And Mann's logic was simple: a democracy full of voters who can't read is a democracy that's easy to fool.

So he pushed for "common schools": free, public, and paid for by taxes. He wanted trained teachers, longer school years, and better buildings. He called education "the great equalizer of the conditions of men."

Not everyone loved it. Rich families didn't want to pay taxes to educate poor kids. Farmers needed their kids working. And Catholic families noticed that these "public" schools taught from a Protestant Bible, which is one big reason Catholic parish schools spread across the country.

But Mann mostly won. So the next time you're sitting in class wondering who to blame for all this? It's this guy. Horace Mann.
