In the 1830s and 1840s, the United States caught a fever of reform: temperance, asylum reform, public schools, women's rights, utopian communities, and the fight to abolish slavery. This is the Antebellum Reform Era, explained from Charles Finney's revival tents to David Walker's Appeal and William Lloyd Garrison's The Liberator.

It starts in December 1829, when sixty copies of a small pamphlet arrive in Savannah, Georgia, and scare an entire state. What was in it, who wrote it, and why did it hit like a bomb? To answer that, we zoom out to the Second Great Awakening and the Burned-Over District of upstate New York, where the idea took hold that if you can perfect yourself, you can perfect society.
