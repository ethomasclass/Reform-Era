# Style test: what to look at

## Files

- `Style_Test_720p.mp4` (94 s): the same three moments in two looks, voiced at the fast pace.
  1. Cold open, "December, 1829…" to "A pamphlet."
  2. Dix: "Then she took her notes…" to "…lashed into obedience."
  3. Temperance: "Here's a number that sounds made up…" to "…drink today."
- `Style_Frames.jpg`: key frames from both looks on one sheet.
- `pace/`: the cold open (209 words) at four speeds, all from one recording.

## Look A: modern archival collage

- Paper background, black-and-white cut-outs with torn edges, and halftone on ember orange.
- Anton headlines, and hand-drawn marker loops and strikes.
- The highlighter sweeps across the real 1843 Dix memorial as you read it.
- Name cards are orange and black blocks.
- Vocabulary would use the same label style.

## Look B: cinematic documentary

- Near-black background, full-frame graded paintings and daguerreotypes with slow pushes, grain and vignette.
- Headlines and quotes in the light Fraunces serif.
- Short fragments ("Not guns." / "A pamphlet.") replace the captions for emphasis.
- The Dix quote lights up word by word in sync with the narration.
- Embers drift off the hidden pages, a first try at the spark motif.

The plan I recommended mixes the two: look A for the lighter chapters, look B for Dix, Oneida and abolition.

The cold open is the slowest section in the script, because it is full of short fragments ("Not guns. Not gunpowder.").
At the same settings, Video 2's regular prose read about 16% faster, so setting 4 should average about 200 wpm across the full script.
